Description

Siegel Family Endowment funds work at the intersections of Learning, Workforce, and Infrastructure, aiming to understand and shape technology's impact on society through inquiry-driven grantmaking. Note: SFE does NOT accept unsolicited proposals — funding follows a 3–12 month relationship, and SFE staff often author the proposal on the grantee's behalf. Announced $14M in grants in May 2026.

Siegel is a prestigious, well-resourced tech-and-society funder whose $250K inquiry-driven grants fit ETC's applied-research model, but the mission overlap is partial and, critically, SFE only funds after a months-long cultivated relationship it does not currently have with ETC. Treat this as a long-game relationship-building target—identify a warm introduction and frame ETC's labor-rights/trust-infrastructure work against SFE's Workforce/Infrastructure pillars—rather than a near-term application worth heavy proposal effort.

Mission alignment55/100 · weight 30%

SFE's tech-and-society lens is broadly compatible, but its three pillars (Learning, Workforce, Infrastructure) don't map cleanly to ETC's humanitarian core (migration, forced labor, disaster, heritage). The strongest bridges are labor rights under Workforce and trust/digital infrastructure under Infrastructure.

Funding fit70/100 · weight 20%

$250K is a healthy award size and SFE's inquiry-driven model supports applied research and staff time rather than pure overhead, which fits ETC's needs well.

Competitiveness30/100 · weight 20%

SFE does not accept unsolicited proposals and funds only after a 3–12 month relationship; ETC has no established tie to SFE, so realistic near-term competitiveness is low despite strong NYU/Microsoft backing.

Effort / feasibility45/100 · weight 15%

SFE staff often author the proposal, lowering writing burden, but the required multi-month relationship-cultivation process is a real time cost with uncertain payoff for a bandwidth-constrained team.
