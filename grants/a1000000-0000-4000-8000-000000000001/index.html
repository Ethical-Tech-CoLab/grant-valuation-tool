Description

The Mozilla Technology Fund (MTF) supports projects that provide tools and resources to auditors working to create greater accountability for AI systems. Winners contribute to the Open Source Audit Tooling (OAT) Initiative (led by Mozilla Fellow Deb Raji). Mozilla defines 'audit tools' broadly: benchmarks/datasets, analysis tools, crowdsourcing tools, documentation templates, and frameworks. Applications typically open in a September–October window each cycle; all outputs must be open source.

Strategy

This is a reach grant: Mozilla's MTF funds narrowly-scoped, open-source AI audit tools that plug into Deb Raji's OAT Initiative, and ETC's humanitarian intervention model only fits if you reframe an existing project as a concrete auditing tool for a human-rights use case. Your one credible entry point is the Traceability project (verifiable ethical claims in supply chains) recast as an open-source tool that lets auditors detect forced-labor or greenwashing in algorithmic/supply-chain claims — do NOT propose a broad platform. Only pursue if you can commit to a working prototype, a 12-month cohort obligation (monthly calls + MozFest), and fully open-source/CC release; if partner deployment models require closed code, walk away now rather than burn writing bandwidth on a $50K long shot.

Tips, most important first

  • highPositioningReframe Traceability as an auditor-facing tool, not a supply-chain platform

    MTF funds tools FOR auditors creating accountability, not intervention systems. Recast your supply-chain traceability work as a benchmark/dataset or analysis tool that lets labor-rights auditors verify or falsify a company's ethical-sourcing claims — a concrete, reusable audit artifact in the spirit of the 2023 'observatory' and pricing-inspection tools.

  • highPitfall to avoidResolve the open-source conflict before you write a word

    Every line of code must ship open source and content under Creative Commons — this collides with the closed partner/agency deployment models ETC often uses. Confirm with your intended partner (and Microsoft Research collaborators) that the specific audit tool can be fully OSS, or the proposal is dead on arrival.

  • highFunder researchAnchor explicitly to the OAT Initiative and its people

    Winners must contribute to the Open Source Audit Tooling Initiative led by Deb Raji (with Birhane, Vecchione, Steed, Ojewale). Read OAT's published standards and name how your tool plugs into them — reviewers reward interoperability with the existing community, not a standalone artifact.

  • highPositioningMatch the $50K scope: one shippable tool, not a research agenda

    The 2022–2023 cohorts funded tightly-scoped, shippable tools (crowdsourced voice-assistant bias measurement, a content-promotion observatory). Scope your deliverable to something a small team can build and release in 12 months, and lead with the specific accountability decision it enables for a named auditor type.

  • highProposal craftLead with a working prototype, not a concept

    MTF applicants 'generally need a working prototype.' Show a demonstrable slice of your traceability tooling now and evidence your interdisciplinary team (policy + technical prototyping, CGA + Microsoft Research) can execute over 12 months — this is where ETC's applied-prototyping strength is your real differentiator over pure-tech applicants.

  • mediumRelationshipUse the pre-deadline window to test fit with Mozilla/OAT

    Applications open ~Sept 6 for an Oct 5 close. Before then, engage the OAT community (public channels, MozFest presence, Deb Raji's published work) to sanity-check whether a forced-labor/traceability audit tool is welcome — this de-risks a low-probability bet and signals you're a genuine community contributor.

  • mediumProcess & logisticsPlan for the two-stage Fluxx flow and cohort commitment

    This is LOI → invited full application via Fluxx, with monthly virtual convenings and mandatory MozFest attendance. Confirm a named team member can carry the cohort obligations for a full year before committing, and map backward from the Oct 5 LOI so the invited full app isn't rushed.

  • mediumBudgetFrame the $50K around researcher/build time, honestly

    The award is small and NYU overhead could swallow it — structure the budget toward developer/researcher time to build and release the open tool, and confirm ETC's constraint of funding staff time over pure overhead can be honored within Mozilla's allowable costs.

  • mediumPitfall to avoidBe willing to defer if no concrete tool concept is ready

    With fit at 49/100 and win probability ~15%, this is only worth it if a specific migration/forced-labor/traceability audit-tool concept is already crystallized. If it isn't by early September, skip this cycle — MTF runs annually, so a stronger prototype next year beats a thin one now.
