Description

Announced January 2026 by the Doris Duke Foundation with Mozilla, Hewlett, and Ford ($6.5M committed toward an $11M three-year goal). AMT centers performing artists in technology innovation across three pillars: direct artist support; infrastructure building via 'AMT Pathways' (artists work with Mozilla engineers on legal/technical/institutional tools — e.g. Responsible Computing Challenge, Mozilla Common Voice); and knowledge building.

Criteria breakdown

Mission alignment15/100 · weight 30%

AMT centers performing artists in technology innovation — arts and creative technology. This has essentially no overlap with ETC's humanitarian and human-rights focus (migration, forced labor, disaster response, cultural heritage return, traceability). The one adjacency is 'responsible computing,' which is far too thin to establish fit.

Funding fit30/100 · weight 20%

The program does fund infrastructure and knowledge building, which could nominally cover applied work, but its dollars are structured around direct artist support and artist-engineer pathways — not researcher/staff time for humanitarian tech prototyping. Award size is unspecified, adding uncertainty.

Competitiveness8/100 · weight 20%

ETC is not a performing-arts organization and does not center artists; the applicant pool will be artist collectives, arts nonprofits, and creative technologists. ETC's NYU CGA + Microsoft Research backing is irrelevant to this funder's selection criteria, making it highly uncompetitive.

Effort / feasibility40/100 · weight 15%

No deadline or clear application process is given, and ETC would have to heavily contort its work to fit an artist-centered frame — a poor use of limited grant-writing bandwidth for a low-probability bid.

Strategic value25/100 · weight 15%

A Mozilla/Ford/Hewlett relationship has some abstract value in responsible-computing circles, but pursuing an off-mission arts grant would not build reputation or capabilities that compound for ETC's core humanitarian projects.

Red flags

  • Program explicitly centers performing artists — ETC is not an arts organization and does not work with performing artists
  • Core AMT pillars (direct artist support, artist-engineer pathways) do not map to ETC's applied humanitarian-tech research and prototyping model
  • No overlap with any of ETC's focus areas or current projects (evacuation, cultural heritage return, traceability, diplomacy)
  • No stated award size or deadline, making planning and ROI assessment difficult
  • Likely eligibility mismatch: applicants expected to be artists or arts-serving organizations
