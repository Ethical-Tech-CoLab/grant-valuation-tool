The topical fit is excellent—this is squarely ETC's forced-labor and supply-chain traceability terrain—but the grant is a large federal implementation cooperative agreement, not an applied-research/prototyping award, and ILAB overwhelmingly funds experienced international-development implementers with field presence. ETC should not pursue this as a lead; instead explore joining a lead implementer's consortium as the traceability/tech-prototyping partner, where its NYU CGA + Microsoft Research credibility adds distinctive value at manageable effort.

Criteria breakdown

Mission alignment90/100 · weight 30%

Child/forced labor in critical mineral supply chains maps almost perfectly onto ETC's forced-labor and supply-chain traceability focus areas and its verifiable-ethical-claims work.

Funding fit40/100 · weight 20%

ILAB's $9M cooperative agreements typically fund multi-year, field-based program implementation (monitoring, capacity-building in producer countries), not applied research and prototyping; the size and structure poorly match ETC's researcher/staff-time and prototype model.

Competitiveness25/100 · weight 20%

ILAB awards overwhelmingly go to established international-development implementers (ILO, Verité, Winrock, Solidarity Center) with on-the-ground presence and past DOL performance; ETC would be uncompetitive as a lead but plausible as a tech/traceability subrecipient.

Effort / feasibility25/100 · weight 15%

Federal cooperative agreements demand heavy proposals—detailed logframes, monitoring/evaluation plans, extensive budgets, past-performance narratives—and onerous reporting, straining ETC's limited grant-writing bandwidth.

Strategic value55/100 · weight 15%

A federal DOL award would meaningfully elevate ETC's forced-labor/traceability portfolio and open institutional relationships, but the strategic upside is more realistic through a partnering role than a solo lead.

Red flags

  • Scope mismatch: ILAB funds large-scale international program implementation and field monitoring, not lab-to-field prototyping—likely requires on-the-ground operations in mineral-producing countries outside ETC's wheelhouse.
  • $9M award size implies extensive field staffing and subgrantee management ETC is not structured to lead.
  • Eligibility and scoring strongly favor organizations with prior DOL/ILAB past performance and international field presence; ETC has none as lead.
  • Heavy federal application and compliance/reporting burden relative to ETC's bandwidth.
